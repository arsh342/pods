Pods Privacy Policy

Effective 28 November 2026

Summary

Pods is an Android app for using and controlling AirPods, published by the developer arsh342. This policy covers the Pods app (package com.arsh.pods).

  • The developer does not collect, receive, sell or share your personal data.
  • Pods does not request Android's internet permission and has no server of its own.
  • There are no accounts, adverts, analytics or tracking tools.
  • The information Pods needs to work is processed on your phone and exchanged with your AirPods over Bluetooth.

Information Pods uses on your device

AirPods details
Name, model, serial numbers, firmware and hardware versions, Bluetooth address, battery and charging state, in-ear state and settings. Used to connect to your AirPods, show their status and change their settings.
Pairing keys
Keys provided by your AirPods, used to recognise them and read their encrypted Bluetooth status messages.
Nearby Bluetooth signals
Pods scans for Bluetooth signals to find your AirPods and read their battery level. It does not use these scans to work out your location, and it does not keep a history of nearby devices.
Your phone's Bluetooth address and connected devices
Where Android allows access, Pods reads your phone's Bluetooth address, and it receives the addresses of other devices connected to your AirPods. These are used to move audio between devices.
Head movement
Motion readings from your AirPods are used for head gestures and the head tracking view. They are processed as they arrive and are not saved.
Hearing settings
If you use hearing aid or transparency adjustments, including hearing test values you enter, Pods writes them to your AirPods. Pods does not keep its own copy.
Phone calls
Pods checks whether a call is ringing, active or ended, so it can move audio to your AirPods and let you answer or decline with a gesture. It does not read phone numbers, contacts or call history, and it does not record or listen to calls.
Media playback and phone battery
Pods checks whether media is playing, and may pause it, resume it or change the volume. It reads your phone's battery level if you show it in the widget.
App preferences
Your choices, such as button actions, equalizer settings and widget appearance.
Diagnostic messages
Pods writes technical messages, which can include Bluetooth addresses, to Android's system log on your phone. Pods does not send this log anywhere.

Android permissions

  • Nearby devices (Bluetooth): find, connect to and control your AirPods.
  • Notifications: show the connection status and low battery alerts.
  • Phone: detect calls and answer or decline them when you ask.
  • Run in the background and at startup: stay connected to your AirPods and keep widgets current.
  • Audio settings: adjust audio behaviour while your AirPods are in use.
  • System-level Bluetooth and phone permissions: declared for devices that grant them, such as rooted phones. Android does not grant them to a normal installation.

On rooted phones where you grant root access, Pods can use it to read the phone's Bluetooth address and to enable features that standard Android does not allow.

You can withdraw permissions in your phone's Settings. Features that depend on them will stop working.

When information leaves the app

  • Your AirPods: settings and device information are exchanged with them over Bluetooth.
  • Android and other software on your phone: Pods announces AirPods status, such as battery level, name and connection, so that system features and widgets can show it. Other apps on your phone with Bluetooth access may be able to receive these announcements.
  • Android backup: if backup is switched on, Android may copy Pods' saved settings, including the AirPods details and pairing keys above, to your Google Account or to a new phone. Google handles this under its own privacy policy. The developer cannot access these backups. You can manage backup in your phone's Settings.
  • Google Play: the Google Play version may ask Google Play to show a rating prompt. Anything you submit there goes to Google, not through Pods.

Google's practices are described in the Google Privacy Policy.

Storage, security and deletion

Saved information is kept in Pods' private app storage, which Android protects from other apps on a normal phone. It stays there until you delete it.

To delete it, open your phone's Settings, choose Apps, then Pods, then Storage, then Clear data. Uninstalling Pods also removes its data from the phone. To remove a backup copy, use your Google Account's backup settings.

Because the developer holds none of your data, there is nothing for the developer to access, correct or delete on request.

Children

Pods is not directed at children and does not collect personal data from anyone, including children.

Changes to this policy

If Pods changes how it handles information, this page will be updated and the effective date above will change.

Contact

For privacy questions, open an issue on the Pods GitHub page. Issues are public, so do not include personal information.
